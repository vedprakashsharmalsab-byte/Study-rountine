## 🌟 1. Fastrack Core Revision & Key Concepts

### Key Definitions:
1. **Line of Sight**: The straight line drawn from the eye of the observer to the point in the object viewed by the observer.
2. **Angle of Elevation**: The angle formed by the line of sight with the horizontal when the object viewed is **above** the horizontal level (observer raises head).
3. **Angle of Depression**: The angle formed by the line of sight with the horizontal when the object viewed is **below** the horizontal level (observer lowers head).

---

## 💡 2. Knowledge Boosters & Golden Rules

- **Rule 1**: The angles of elevation and depression are **always acute** ($0^\circ < \theta < 90^\circ$).
- **Rule 2**: By alternate interior angles, the **angle of depression of an object from observer = angle of elevation of observer from object**.
- **Rule 3**: If the observer moves **towards** the foot of the vertical object, the angle of elevation **increases** (e.g. $30^\circ \to 45^\circ \to 60^\circ$).
- **Rule 4**: If both the height of the tower and the distance between observer and foot of tower are doubled, the angle of elevation **remains unchanged**.

## 🏛️ 3. High-Frequency CBSE Case Studies

### Case Study 1: India Gate Educational Trip
- India Gate monument height $BC = 42\text{ m}$.
- Standing at $42\sqrt{3}\text{ m} \implies \tan\theta = \frac{42}{42\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \implies \theta = 30^\circ$.
- To view at $60^\circ \implies \text{Distance } x = \frac{42}{\sqrt{3}} = 14\sqrt{3} \approx 24.24\text{ m}$.
- Vertical tower casting $30\text{ m}$ shadow at $30^\circ \implies h = 30 \tan 30^\circ = 10\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$.

### Case Study 2: Satellite Tracking Two Mountain Peaks
- Peaks: Kangchenjunga ($8586\text{ m}$) & Mullayanagiri ($1930\text{ m}$).
- Distance between base points $= 2046\text{ km} \implies$ Half distance $= 1023\text{ km}$.
- Kangchenjunga satellite distance $= \frac{1023}{\cos 30^\circ} = \frac{2046}{\sqrt{3}} = 682\sqrt{3} \approx 1181.22\text{ km}$.
- Vertical distance of satellite from ground $= 590.6 + 8.586 = 599.2\text{ km}$.

### Case Study 3: Radio Tower Sections A & B
- Point $O$ is $36\text{ cm}$ from base $P$. Angle to top of B is $30^\circ$, angle to top of A is $45^\circ$.
- Length of wire to B: $OB = \frac{36}{\cos 30^\circ} = 24\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}$.
- Distance $AB = AP - BP = 36 - 12\sqrt{3} = 12(3 - \sqrt{3})\text{ cm}$.
- Area of $\triangle OPB = \frac{1}{2} \times 36 \times 12\sqrt{3} = 216\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}^2$.

### Case Study 4: Kite Festival (Two Kites A & B)
- Hand position $C$, Kite A string angle $30^\circ$ at height $50\text{ m} \implies \text{String length } AC = 100\text{ m}$.
- Kite B string angle $60^\circ$ at height $60\text{ m} \implies \text{String length } BC = 40\sqrt{3}\text{ m}$.
- Direct distance between kites $d = \sqrt{(70\sqrt{3})^2 + 10^2} = \sqrt{14700 + 100} = \sqrt{14800} \approx 121.66\text{ m}$.

### Case Study 5: Gadisar Lake Chhatri Water Reflection
- Chhatri height above water $= 10\text{ m}$.
- Observation point $A$ at height $h$. Elevation of Chhatri $= 45^\circ$, depression of image $= 60^\circ$.
- Image of point $B$ with respect to water surface is at equal distance below water surface ($BE = EC$).
- Solving: $h = \frac{10(\sqrt{3}-1)}{\sqrt{3}+1} = 10(2-\sqrt{3}) \approx 2.7\text{ m}$.

## 🏆 4. Master Proof: Cloud Reflection in Lake

**Theorem / 5-Mark Question**:
> *If the angle of elevation of a cloud from a point $h$ metres above a lake is $\alpha$ and the angle of depression of its reflection in the lake is $\beta$, prove that the height of the cloud above the lake is:*
$$H = \frac{h(\tan\beta + \tan\alpha)}{\tan\beta - \tan\alpha}$$

**Proof**:
1. Let height of cloud above water surface $= x$.
2. Distance of cloud image below water surface $= x$.
3. Let horizontal distance between observer and cloud $= y$.
4. Height of cloud above observer level $= x - h \implies \tan\alpha = \frac{x - h}{y} \implies y = \frac{x - h}{\tan\alpha}$.
5. Depth of cloud reflection below observer level $= x + h \implies \tan\beta = \frac{x + h}{y} \implies y = \frac{x + h}{\tan\beta}$.
6. Equating: $\frac{x - h}{\tan\alpha} = \frac{x + h}{\tan\beta} \implies (x - h)\tan\beta = (x + h)\tan\alpha$.
7. $x(\tan\beta - \tan\alpha) = h(\tan\beta + \tan\alpha) \implies x = \frac{h(\tan\beta + \tan\alpha)}{\tan\beta - \tan\alpha}$. $\blacksquare$
