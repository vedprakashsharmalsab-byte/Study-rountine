## 🌟 1. Core Physics Foundations

### 1. Electric Current ($I$)
- **Definition**: Rate of flow of electric charge through any cross-section of a conductor.
- **Formula**:
  $$I = \frac{Q}{t} \iff Q = I \cdot t$$
- **Quantization of Charge**: $Q = n \cdot e$ where $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$.
  - Number of electrons in $1\text{ Coulomb} = \frac{1}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} = 6.25 \times 10^{18}\text{ electrons}$.
- **SI Unit**: Ampere ($\text{A}$). $1\text{ A} = 1\text{ Coulomb} / \text{second}$.
  - $1\text{ mA} = 10^{-3}\text{ A}$, $1\ \mu\text{A} = 10^{-6}\text{ A}$.
- **Measurement**: **Ammeter** (connected in **series**, has very low resistance).

---

### 2. Electric Potential & Potential Difference ($V$)
- **Definition**: Work done in moving a unit positive charge from one point to another in an electric field.
- **Formula**:
  $$V = \frac{W}{Q} \iff W = V \cdot Q = V \cdot I \cdot t$$
- **SI Unit**: Volt ($\text{V}$). $1\text{ Volt} = 1\text{ Joule} / \text{Coulomb}$.
- **Measurement**: **Voltmeter** (connected in **parallel**, has very high resistance).

## ⚖️ 4. Ohm's Law & Resistance

### Ohm's Law Statement:
> *The potential difference $V$ across the ends of a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the current $I$ passing through it, provided its temperature and physical conditions remain constant.*

$$V \propto I \implies V = I \cdot R \iff R = \frac{V}{I}$$

- **V-I Graph**: Straight line passing through the origin. Slope $= \frac{V}{I} = R$.
- **Factors Affecting Resistance ($R$)**:
  $$R = \rho \frac{l}{A}$$
  1. Directly proportional to length ($l$).
  2. Inversely proportional to area of cross-section ($A$).
  3. Directly proportional to temperature.
  4. Dependent on the nature of material ($\rho$, Resistivity).

- **Resistivity ($\rho$)**:
  - SI Unit: $\Omega \cdot \text{m}$ (Ohm-metre).
  - Depends **only on material and temperature** (independent of shape, length, or thickness!).
  - Metals/Alloys: $10^{-8}$ to $10^{-6}\ \Omega\text{m}$.
  - Insulators: $10^{12}$ to $10^{17}\ \Omega\text{m}$.
  - **Alloys** (e.g. Nichrome) have higher resistivity and do not oxidize/burn readily at high temperatures $\implies$ used in heating appliances.

## 🔗 5. Series vs Parallel Combinations

| Parameter | Series Combination | Parallel Combination (Domestic Choice) |
|---|---|---|
| **Current ($I$)** | Same through all resistors | Divides across branches ($I = I_1 + I_2 + I_3$) |
| **Voltage ($V$)** | Divides ($V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3$) | Same across all branches |
| **Equivalent Resistance** | $R_s = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + \dots$ | $\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3} + \dots$ |
| **Resultant Value** | Greater than the greatest resistor | Lesser than the least resistor |
| **Failure Impact** | One fails $\implies$ Entire circuit breaks | One fails $\implies$ Other branches continue working |

## 🔥 6. Heating Effect of Current & Electric Power

### Joule's Law of Heating:
$$H = W = V \cdot I \cdot t = I^2 R t = \frac{V^2 t}{R}$$

- **Applications**:
  - **Electric Bulb Filament**: Made of **Tungsten** (high melting point $3380^\circ\text{C}$, does not oxidize); filled with inactive gases (**Nitrogen & Argon**).
  - **Electric Fuse**: Safety device made of pure tin or alloy of copper & tin (low melting point); always connected in **series with live wire**.

### Electric Power ($P$):
$$P = \frac{W}{t} = V \cdot I = I^2 R = \frac{V^2}{R}$$
- **SI Unit**: Watt ($\text{W}$). $1\text{ W} = 1\text{ Volt} \times 1\text{ Ampere}$.
- **Commercial Unit of Energy**: Kilowatt-hour ($\text{kWh}$) or "Unit":
  $$1\text{ kWh} = 1000\text{ W} \times 3600\text{ s} = 3.6 \times 10^6\text{ J} = 3.6\text{ MJ}$$

## 🧠 7. High-Yield Practice & Case Studies

### Assertion-Reason Mastery:
- **Assertion**: A current-carrying wire should be charged.
- **Reason**: The current in a wire is due to flow of free electrons in a definite direction.
- **Answer**: Assertion is **FALSE**, Reason is **TRUE** (Option D).
- **Explanation**: Even though electrons are moving, the total number of protons inside the wire equals the total number of electrons at any instant, so net electrostatic charge remains ZERO.

### Case Study: Refrigerator Commercial Energy Cost
- Refrigerator rated $400\text{ W}$ operates $8\text{ hours/day}$ for $30\text{ days}$. Cost $= ₹5 / \text{kWh}$.
- Daily Energy $= 400\text{ W} \times 8\text{ h} = 3200\text{ Wh} = 3.2\text{ kWh}$.
- Monthly Energy $= 3.2 \times 30 = 96\text{ kWh}$.
- Total Cost $= 96 \times ₹5 = ₹480$.
