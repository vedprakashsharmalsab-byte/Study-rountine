## 🌟 1. Critical Physical Exceptions

| Property | General Trend | Critical NCERT Exceptions ⭐ |
|---|---|---|
| **Physical State (Metals)** | Solid at room temperature | **Mercury ($\text{Hg}$)** is a liquid metal at room temperature. |
| **Physical State (Non-Metals)** | Solids or Gases | **Bromine ($\text{Br}_2$)** is the ONLY liquid non-metal. |
| **Hardness (Metals)** | Generally hard | **Sodium ($\text{Na}$) and Potassium ($\text{K}$)** are very soft and can be cut with a knife. |
| **Hardness (Non-Metals)** | Generally soft / brittle | **Diamond** (allotrope of Carbon) is the hardest naturally occurring substance. |
| **Melting Point (Metals)** | High melting points | **Gallium ($\text{Ga}$) and Caesium ($\text{Cs}$)** have very low melting points and melt on your palm. |
| **Electrical Conductivity (Non-Metals)** | Poor conductors / Insulators | **Graphite** (allotrope of Carbon) is a good conductor of electricity. |
| **Lustre (Non-Metals)** | Dull appearance | **Iodine** is a non-metal but has a metallic lustre / shine. |
| **Thermal Conductivity (Metals)** | Good conductors of heat | **Lead ($\text{Pb}$) and Mercury ($\text{Hg}$)** are comparatively poor conductors of heat. |
| **Best Electrical Conductors** | Metals | **Silver ($\text{Ag}$)** is the #1 best, followed by **Copper ($\text{Cu}$)**. |
| **Malleability & Ductility** | Metals | **Gold ($\text{Au}$)** is the most ductile (1g can draw 2km wire) and malleable. |

## 💥 2. Chemical Properties & Oxygen Reactions

### 1. Reaction with Oxygen ($\text{Metal} + \text{Oxygen} \to \text{Metal Oxide}$)
- **$\text{Na}$ & $\text{K}$**: React vigorously, catching fire in open air $\implies$ stored under **kerosene oil**.
- **Magnesium ($\text{Mg}$)**: Burns with a **dazzling white flame**:
  $$2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{MgO}$$
- **Aluminium ($\text{Al}$)**: Forms a thin protective layer of $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ preventing further oxidation (anodising principle).
- **Iron ($\text{Fe}$)**: Iron rod does not burn, but **iron filings** burn vigorously when sprinkled in a flame.
- **Copper ($\text{Cu}$)**: Does not burn, but on prolonged heating forms a **black coating of Copper(II) Oxide**:
  $$2\text{Cu} + \text{O}_2 \to 2\text{CuO} \quad (\text{Black})$$
- **Silver ($\text{Ag}$) & Gold ($\text{Au}$)**: Do not react with oxygen even at high temperatures.

## ⚖️ 3. Amphoteric Oxides (Acid + Base Dual Reaction)
> *Oxides that react with BOTH acids and bases to produce salt and water.*

1. **Zinc Oxide ($\text{ZnO}$)**:
   - With Acid: $\text{ZnO} + 2\text{HCl} \to \text{ZnCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$
   - With Base: $\text{ZnO} + 2\text{NaOH} \to \text{Na}_2\text{ZnO}_2 \text{ (Sodium Zincate)} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$
2. **Aluminium Oxide ($\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$)**:
   - With Acid: $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 6\text{HCl} \to 2\text{AlCl}_3 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$
   - With Base: $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 2\text{NaOH} \to 2\text{NaAlO}_2 \text{ (Sodium Aluminate)} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$

## 💧 4. Water & Acid Reactivity Exceptions

- **Non-reactive with Water**: $\text{Pb}$, $\text{Cu}$, $\text{Ag}$, $\text{Au}$ do not react with water or steam.
- **Metals Below Hydrogen**: $\text{Cu}$, $\text{Ag}$, $\text{Au}$, $\text{Pt}$ do not liberate $\text{H}_2$ gas from dilute acids.

### ⭐ Nitric Acid ($\text{HNO}_3$) Exception:
- $\text{HNO}_3$ is a **strong oxidizing agent**.
- When metals react with $\text{HNO}_3$, $\text{H}_2$ gas is NOT evolved because $\text{HNO}_3$ oxidizes $\text{H}_2$ into $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ and itself reduces to nitrogen oxides ($\text{NO}_2, \text{NO}, \text{N}_2\text{O}$).
- **CRITICAL NCERT EXCEPTION**: **Magnesium ($\text{Mg}$)** and **Manganese ($\text{Mn}$)** react with **very dilute $\text{HNO}_3$ ($1\%$)** to liberate $\text{H}_2$ gas!

---

## 👑 5. Aqua Regia (Royal Water)
- **Composition**: Freshly prepared mixture of **Concentrated $\text{HCl}$** and **Concentrated $\text{HNO}_3$** in **$3 : 1$ ratio**.
- **Properties**: Highly corrosive, fuming yellow/red liquid.
- **Function**: Can dissolve noble metals like **Gold ($\text{Au}$)** and **Platinum ($\text{Pt}$)** even though neither acid can do so alone.
